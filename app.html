🛡️ Critical Safety Rules
  • ⬜
    Preservative in Aqueous Formula Any formula containing water must include a broad-spectrum preservative system validated against bacteria, yeast, and mould (Challenge Test / Preservative Efficacy Test to ISO 11930).
  • ⬜
    Antioxidant ≠ Preservative Vitamin E, Rosemary Extract, Ferulic Acid protect against oxidative rancidity in oils — they do NOT prevent microbial growth. Do not use in place of a preservative.
  • ⬜
    pH Compatibility Skin pH is 4.5–5.5. Confirm your formula pH is appropriate for your actives (e.g. Vitamin C <3.5, AHAs 3.0–4.0, most preservatives effective 3.5–6.0). Adjust with citric acid (down) or sodium hydroxide (up).
  • ⬜
    Regulatory Usage Limits Check each ingredient against EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 Annexes, UK equivalent, and FDA guidelines. Preservatives and actives have strict maximum concentrations.
  • ⬜
    Fragrance Allergen Declaration — EU 2023/1545 (Deadline: 31 July 2026) The EU allergen list has expanded from 26 to 82 allergens under Regulation (EU) 2023/1545. All 82 must be declared by INCI name if above threshold: ≥0.001% leave-on, ≥0.01% rinse-off. Includes natural essential oils (lavender, bergamot, citrus, ylang ylang, etc.) and their components (Limonene, Linalool, Citronellol, Eugenol, Geraniol, Menthol, Camphor, Vanillin and 74 more). Products placed on market after 31 July 2026 must comply. Products already on market have until 31 July 2028.
  • ⬜
    SPF Claim Requirement Any SPF claim requires inclusion of approved UV filters only (EU Annex VI), SPF testing to ISO 24444 (in vivo) or validated in vitro method, and UVA protection ratio assessment.
  • ⬜
    Nanomaterial Notification If using nano-form ingredients (e.g. Nano Titanium Dioxide), EU law requires notification to the EU Commission 6 months before placing on market.
  • ⬜
    Formula Totals 100% Formula must total exactly 100%. Use distilled water (Aqua) as quantum satis (QS) to make up the difference.

🧫 Preservative Efficacy Guide
ℹ️
Preservative systems must pass the Challenge Test (ISO 11930) — a lab test where your finished product is deliberately inoculated with pathogens to verify the preservative works. No formula with water should reach consumers without this test.
  • 🟢
    AMTicide Coconut + Leucidal Liquid SF Natural fermentation-derived combination. AMTicide covers bacteria, Leucidal covers yeast and mould. Use 2–4% each. COSMOS-approvable. Effective pH 3.5–6.5. Must pass ISO 11930.
  • 🟢
    Benzyl Alcohol + Dehydroacetic Acid Suitable for "natural" branding. Max BA 1.0% EU. Best at pH ≤ 6.
  • 🟡
    Potassium Sorbate + Sodium Benzoate Weak alone — typically a booster only. Must be pH ≤ 5. Cannot be used as sole system.
  • 🔴
    Vitamin E / Rosemary Extract alone These are antioxidants ONLY. They prevent oil rancidity, NOT microbial contamination.

    Key global regulations for cosmetic products — EU, UK, US, and Canada. Always verify with a qualified regulatory consultant before launch.
    🇪🇺 EU / UK Cosmetics Regulation
    • 📄
      EU Regulation 1223/2009Primary EU framework. Includes banned (Annex II), restricted (Annex III), and allowed ingredient lists (Annex IV–VI).
    • 📄
      UK Cosmetics Regulation (2021)Post-Brexit — mirrors EU regulation but separate enforcement via OPSS. Products need UK RP (Responsible Person).
    • ✅
      CPSR (Safety Report)Mandatory. Part A + B. Must be updated if formula changes.
    • ✅
      CPNP NotificationEU: Notify via CPNP portal before market. UK: Notify via SCPN.
    • ✅
      Product Information File (PIF)Must be kept for 10 years. Includes formula, safety report, claims evidence, manufacturing records.
    • ✅
      Responsible Person (RP)A legal EU/UK entity responsible for compliance. Can be the manufacturer or a designee.
    🇺🇸 US FDA / MoCRA
    • 📄
      Modernisation of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA) 2022New FDA authority. Mandatory facility registration and product listing. Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) rules coming.
    • ✅
      FDA Facility RegistrationRequired for all cosmetic manufacturers/processors. Annual renewal.
    • ✅
      Serious Adverse Event ReportingMandatory reporting of serious adverse events to FDA within 15 business days.
    • ⚠️
      No pre-market approvalUnlike drugs, cosmetics don't need FDA approval — but labelling and ingredient safety are the manufacturer's legal responsibility.
    • ✅
      FD&C Act + Fair Packaging & Labelling ActINCI declaration required. Net weight, distributor name and address mandatory.
    🏷️ Mandatory Label Requirements (EU/UK)
    Required on Pack
    • Name & address of Responsible Person
    • Country of origin (if outside EU/UK)
    • Nominal content (weight/volume)
    • Date of minimum durability (PAO or BBE)
    • Precautions of use
    • Batch number
    • Function of product
    • INCI ingredient list
    PAO vs Best Before
    • PAO (Period After Opening) = open jar symbol + months e.g. "12M" — required if >30 months shelf life
    • BBE (Best Before End) = hourglass symbol + date — required if ≤30 months shelf life
    • Both determined by stability testing
    🌸 Fragrance Allergen Declaration — 2026 Update
    • 82 allergens must now be declared (EU 2023/1545)
    • Leave-on threshold: ≥ 0.001%
    • Rinse-off threshold: ≥ 0.01%
    • Deadline for new products: 31 July 2026
    • Deadline for existing stock: 31 July 2028
    • Includes natural EOs: lavender, bergamot, citrus, ylang ylang oils
    • Grouped allergens declared under single INCI name (e.g. Citral covers Geranial + Neral)
    🌸 Full EU Allergen Reference — Annex III (EU 2023/1545)
    ⚠️
    Deadline: 31 July 2026. All products placed on the EU/UK market after this date must declare any of these 82 allergens present above threshold. Many are found in natural essential oils and botanical extracts — not just synthetic fragrances. Check with your fragrance/EO supplier for allergen content declarations.
    Original 26 Allergens (pre-2023, always required)
    56 New Allergens — Required from 31 July 2026
    🍁 Canada — Health Canada Cosmetic Regulations
    🍁
    Regulatory framework: Cosmetics in Canada are regulated under the Food and Drugs Act (FDA) and the Cosmetic Regulations (C.R.C., c. 869), last amended October 2024 (SOR/2024-63). Canada has deliberately harmonised with EU cosmetic standards — the allergen scanner already built into CEdermalab covers Canada's requirements because Health Canada's allergen list is identical to the EU's.
    📋 Key Requirements
    • 📄
      Cosmetic Notification Form (CNF) Every cosmetic product must be notified to Health Canada within 10 days of first sale in Canada. Submit via Health Canada's online portal. The CNF must include the full ingredient list, product category, and — from April 2026 — any fragrance allergens present above threshold. This is a mandatory legal requirement, not optional.
    • 🍁
      Canadian Responsible Person (RP) As of the 2024 amendments, a Responsible Person located in Canada must be designated for every cosmetic product placed on the Canadian market. This person is legally accountable for regulatory compliance. International brands must appoint a Canadian RP — this can be a regulatory consulting firm with Canadian offices.
    • 🗂️
      Records Retention — 6 Years Maintain detailed records of cosmetic formulations and provide them to Health Canada upon request. Records must be kept for a minimum of 6 years from the date the product was last sold. The Lab Notebook in CEdermalab supports this — export your formula notes and stability records for your records file.
    • 🚫
      Cosmetic Ingredient Hotlist Health Canada's Cosmetic Ingredient Hotlist (last updated February 2025) identifies prohibited and restricted substances in cosmetics sold in Canada. It functions similarly to the EU's Annex II and Annex III lists. Check all ingredients against the current Hotlist at canada.ca before placing any product on the Canadian market. Notable 2025 additions include restrictions on Retinol concentrations and Polyaminopropyl Biguanide.
    🏷️ Canadian Label Requirements
    • 🇨🇦
      Bilingual Labelling — English & French All mandatory label information must appear in both English and French for products sold in Canada. This applies to product identity, net quantity, warnings, and directions for use. INCI ingredient names, where identical in both languages, require only one listing. This is a distinct requirement not found in EU or US regulations.
    • 📋
      INCI Ingredient List Full INCI ingredient list required, in descending order of concentration. Ingredients at 1% or less may be listed in any order after those above 1%. CEdermalab generates this automatically. Fragrance allergens (from April 2026) must appear after "Fragrance (Parfum)" individually by INCI name if above threshold.
    • ⚠️
      Mandatory Warnings Any ingredient on the Hotlist with a restriction (rather than prohibition) may require a specific cautionary statement on the label — e.g. "Do not use in the area of the eye, mouth or nose." Check the current Hotlist for applicable statements for every restricted ingredient in your formula.
    • 📦
      Small Package Exemption Where a container is too small to carry a legible ingredient list, the list may be provided on an attached tag, tape, or card — or on a website, with a bilingual statement on the label directing consumers to the web address.
    🌸 Canada Fragrance Allergen Disclosure Timeline — SOR/2024-63
    ⚠️
    Health Canada's fragrance allergen list is identical to the EU's. The allergen scanner in CEdermalab therefore covers Canadian requirements in full. Thresholds are identical: ≥0.001% leave-on, ≥0.01% rinse-off. The key difference is Canada's phased timeline and bilingual labelling.
    Phase 1
    12 April 2026
    24 core allergens — all new and existing products must declare these if present above threshold. Same as original EU list of 26 (minor grouping differences).
    Phase 2
    1 August 2026
    Full 81 allergens — new products only must comply with the expanded EU-equivalent list. Existing products have until August 2028.
    Phase 3
    1 August 2028
    Full 81 allergens — all products. All cosmetics on the Canadian market must comply with the complete allergen disclosure requirements.
    🏷️ How allergens must appear on a Canadian label
    Fragrance allergens must be listed after "Fragrance (Parfum)" in the ingredient list, alongside all other ingredients. The INCI name is used. Where the INCI name is the same in English and French, one listing is sufficient. Grouped allergens (e.g. Rose Flower Oil/Extract covering multiple rose-derived substances) are declared under the group name when the sum of all substances in the group exceeds the threshold.
    Example — AFTER April 12, 2026:
    Water, Glycerin, ..., Fragrance (Parfum), Linalool, Citronellol, ...

    🩹 Consumer Safety — Instructions for Use & Patch Test Requirement
    ✅
    All finished products must be supplied with clear instructions for use, including a mandatory skin allergy patch test to be administered by the consumer before first use of any new product.
    Even products formulated with entirely natural, botanical, and plant-derived ingredients carry a risk of skin sensitisation or allergic reaction in susceptible individuals. Consumer safety must be projected beyond the point of sale. Providing clear instructions for use — including a pre-use patch test — is both an ethical obligation and, in many markets, a regulatory expectation under the duty of care owed by the manufacturer or responsible person.
    Recommended Patch Test Label Copy
    "Before first use, apply a small amount of this product to the inside of your wrist or the skin behind your ear. Leave unwashed for 24–48 hours. If you experience any redness, itching, swelling, burning, or irritation during or after this period, do not use this product and seek advice from a healthcare professional or dermatologist. Even natural and botanical ingredients can cause skin reactions in sensitive individuals. Keep out of reach of children. Avoid contact with eyes."
    Where to include patch test instructions:
    🏷️ Product Label Required on-pack or on an attached leaflet. Must be legible and in the language of the country of sale. EU/UK: mandatory precautions of use under Regulation (EC) 1223/2009.
    🛒 Online Product Listing Include in the product description on your website, Etsy, Amazon, or any marketplace listing. Customers purchasing online must have access to the same safety information as in-store buyers.
    📦 In-Box Insert A printed insert card inside every order is best practice — especially for products sold without outer packaging or where label space is limited. Also provides an opportunity to reinforce your brand values around safety.
    ℹ️
    Products intended for use around the eyes, on children under 3, during pregnancy, or on broken or compromised skin require additional warnings specific to those populations. Always consult your qualified cosmetic safety assessor when formulating for these groups.
